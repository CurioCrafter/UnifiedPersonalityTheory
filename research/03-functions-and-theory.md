# The theory beneath the four letters: Jung, cognitive functions, type dynamics, and the alternatives

Research strand 03. Compiled 2026-10-31 from web searches and page fetches. Many journal pages were paywalled or blocked, so several items rest on abstracts, search snippets or secondary summaries. Each source is labelled in the Sources list.

## Bottom line

The four-letter MBTI scales have decent measurement support, and they map onto four of the Big Five traits. The theory layered on top (a fixed ordered stack of cognitive functions, "type dynamics") is a post-Jung development, and I found no published test that supports it. The only direct tests I located, by Reynierse and colleagues, argue against it, and the Foundation and publisher pages I read cite no validation data for it. Instruments that measure Jung's functions directly (SL-TDI, Grey-Wheelwright) exist but have thin, mostly small-sample psychometric literature. Neuroscience claims (Nardi's EEG work) rest on an unpublished-in-peer-review pilot of roughly 56-65 people. Socionics has almost no independent evidence. 16Personalities is a different model (Big-Five-derived, no functions) whose reliability figures come from the company itself.

## Findings

### 1. What Jung proposed, and what Briggs and Myers changed

- Jung (1921) described two attitudes (extraversion, introversion) and four functions (thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition), giving eight types. He described a dominant function, often modified by one auxiliary, with the opposite function repressed into the unconscious. A secondary summary of the book does not describe a fixed ordered four-slot "stack". [Moderate: secondary summary only; Wikipedia page's own empirical section is flagged as poorly sourced] (Source 11)
- Myers and Briggs added the Judging/Perceiving scale. In their scheme, J/P tells which function a person uses in the outer world, so it marks the dominant function for extraverts and the auxiliary for introverts. [Strong as a description of the instrument's design, via Source 10 citing Myers & Myers 1995]
- The explicit four-slot stack (dominant, auxiliary, tertiary, inferior, alternating direction) was elaborated later: Myers 1962 on dominant/auxiliary, and Grant, Brownsword and others in the 1980s for the full stack. This is from a Truity company blog, so it is a weak source for the history. [Weak] (Source 9)
- Eysenck (1995, as relayed by Wikipedia) argued Jung's scheme implies 32 types, half of which a questionnaire cannot measure, so the 16-type MBTI is incomplete. [Weak: secondary only] (Source 10)
- Jung himself did not endorse a questionnaire; whether the MBTI faithfully operationalizes Jung is disputed. McCrae and Costa (1989) concluded Jung's theory is either wrong or inadequately captured by the MBTI. [Contested] (Source 1)

### 2. Cognitive functions (Ni, Te, etc.) and the function stack as measurable things

- The MBTI scales do not measure functions directly. Function labels are derived from the four letters by rule. The Myers & Briggs Foundation page describes the order from the first and last letters and cites no studies for it. [Untested by the publisher; page is publisher/certifier material] (Source 8)
- McCrae and Costa (1989, n = 468, ages 19-93): E-I correlated about -0.74 with NEO Extraversion, S-N about 0.72 with Openness, T-F about 0.44 with Agreeableness, J-P about -0.49 with Conscientiousness (figures via Wikipedia's summary; the abstract itself says four of five NEO dimensions are captured and the data favor four continuous dimensions over types). Neuroticism has no MBTI counterpart. [Strong for the Big Five overlap, which has been replicated in many samples, though I read only this one primary abstract] (Sources 1, 10)
- Girelli and Stake (1993; 106 women, 59 men, students) compared the standard forced-choice MBTI with a Likert version rating each pole separately. Bipolarity got weak support for E-I and none for S-N and T-F. They attributed the strong negative within-scale correlations to the forced-choice format. [Moderate: abstract-level secondhand; single study, I did not see further replications in my searches] (Source 6)
- Stein and Swan (2019) argue MBTI theory lacks agreement with known data, lacks testability and has internal contradictions (wording taken from a secondary quote I could not check against the paper). [Contested: one critical review, not rebutted in the sources I saw] (Source 7)

### 3. Type dynamics: has it been tested?

- Reynierse and Harker (2008), "Preference Multidimensionality and the Fallacy of Type Dynamics", published in two parts (Studies 1-3 and 4-6) in the Journal of Psychological Type (which is published by the Myers & Briggs Foundation community, so not a hostile outlet). They argue the apparent dynamics effects reduce to additive effects of the individual preferences. I could confirm the existence and thesis only from forum/wiki listings. [Weak: the thesis is confirmed only from secondary community pages] (Sources 12, 13)
- Reynierse (2009), "The Case Against Type Dynamics", reported (per Wikipedia) that the predicted order of function strength appeared in only 1 of 540 test results. Wikipedia's summary also notes Reynierse called type dynamics "fundamentally based on a series of category mistakes". [Weak: figure seen only in a secondary summary, not in the paper] (Source 10)
- Nardi and Rowan published rebuttals in the Foundation's Type journals (per the community wiki). [Contested] (Source 13)
- A general guide I saw says one analysis of California Psychological Inventory scores by type roughly matched the dominant/auxiliary predictions but not the third and fourth functions. I could not trace that to a primary source. [Weak/unverified]
- Reynolds, Hamer, and Bess and Harvey: I could not find a type-dynamics test by "Reynolds" or "Hamer"; "Reynierse" appears to be the researcher meant for the former (see Could not verify). Bess, Harvey and Swartz (2003) is a SIOP conference-proceedings paper on hierarchical confirmatory factor analysis of the MBTI (a scale-structure analysis, not a stack test); I saw only its citation and could not read it. [Untested for stack order by that paper] (Source 14)
- Overall for the full ordered stack: I found no independent peer-reviewed positive test, and the one author who tested it directly reported negative results. [Contested, leaning Untested-in-support]

### 4. Instruments that measure functions directly

- **Singer-Loomis Type Deployment Inventory (SL-TDI)**: scores eight Jungian types/functions on a Likert-style basis. Lee and Kim (2024, Frontiers in Psychology), n = 521 Korean adults after exclusions from 530 online respondents: overall Cronbach's alpha 0.94; type-level alphas 0.61 to 0.76 (IF lowest at 0.61, IN highest at 0.76). A 4-dimension MDS solution correlated with the designed dimensions at E/I 0.47, F/T 0.376, J/P 0.336, S/N 0.192. S/N was not recovered in the higher-distress subgroup (r = 0.144, not significant). The abstract and conclusion disagree about whether the results support a bipolar assumption. Convenience sample, skewed young and female, collected during COVID-19. [Weak: single study, one country] (Source 2, full text read)
- Arnau, Rosen and Thompson (2000), per search summary, reported good reliability and validity for SL-TDI scores; I could not read the full text. [Weak: abstract-level secondhand] (Source 15)
- **Grey-Wheelwright**: I found no usable psychometric source. [Untested here] (Could not verify)
- **Keirsey Temperament Sorter**: Keirsey groups types into four temperaments (a departure from Myers's function-based grouping), according to Wikipedia. The only validity datum I saw is a 2001 Journal of Career Assessment study (n = 203 college freshmen) finding positive correlations between the online KTS II and the MBTI. [Weak: Wikipedia summary of one study] (Source 16)

### 5. Neuroscience claims

- Nardi's work (EEG with a 20-site cap, a roughly one-hour protocol of 20 tasks, since 2006; the book describes it as a pilot of about 65 young adults across all 16 types per a bookseller listing, with reader reviews saying 56-60) is published as a trade book (Neuroscience of Personality, 2011), not as a peer-reviewed paper that I could find. Critics note EEG cannot see deep structures. [Weak: single lab, small sample, non-peer-reviewed venue] (Source 17, search-result detail only)
- Haier: I found no Haier study that links brain imaging to MBTI types. His work I saw was PET glucose metabolism and abstract reasoning (the neural efficiency hypothesis). Park et al. (2010) PET work used Cloninger's inventory, not MBTI, and Deckersbach et al. used NEO-FFI extraversion/neuroticism. These are personality-brain studies but not tests of Jungian functions. [Untested for MBTI functions] (Source 18, search snippets)
- A secondary essay claims late-1990s qEEG studies found brain-wave differences between Jungian preferences; the essay cites no primary source I could verify. [Untested/unverified]

### 6. MBTI Step II facets

- Step II adds 20 facets, five per dichotomy, scored from the item pool. Per Wikipedia (flagged there as needing a citation), it was developed in 1989 as Form K / Expanded Analysis. (Source 10)
- Psychometrics Canada (2008, a publisher-affiliated distributor of the instrument) describes second-order factor analysis resulting in four factors with five facets each loading as expected (attributed to Quenk et al. 2001), and good fit with measurement invariance across English and French samples. I could not extract the numbers from the PDF; this comes from a search-result summary. [Weak: publisher-side and I did not see indices] (Source 5)
- Independent Step II psychometrics and the Step II manual: not accessed.

### 7. Socionics

- Developed from the 1970s by Aušra Augustinavičiūtė in Lithuania, combining Jung with Kępiński's information metabolism, with eight information elements; relies on interview and observation rather than questionnaires, per Blutner and Hochnadel (2010) as reported by Wikipedia. J and P do not mean the same thing as in the MBTI. (Source 19)
- Evidence: most supportive studies come from a small group of authors in socionics-linked Russian journals (e.g., Kovalenko and Zvonareva correlations with Big Five Extraversion and others; a 2022 Russian Journal of Education and Psychology temperament study). Critics (Bogomaz 2000, Monastyrsky 2015) say there is little experimental verification, and the Russian Academy of Sciences' Commission on Pseudoscience has listed it. I saw no independent replications. [Weak for support; Contested overall] (Sources 19, 20)

### 8. Official MBTI vs 16Personalities (NERIS) vs community typology

- **Official MBTI** (The Myers-Briggs Company, paid, certified practitioners): four scales, 16 types, functions derived from the letters. The Foundation page cites no data for type dynamics. The company states the instrument was never meant to predict job performance. (Sources 8, 10)
- **16Personalities (NERIS)**: five scales (Mind, Energy, Nature, Tactics, Identity, the last being Assertive vs Turbulent). Company text says the scales are a reworked Big Five, and it drops Jungian functions as hard to validate. The company's own page reports internal-consistency alphas of 0.87 (I/E), 0.78 (Observant/Intuitive), 0.75 (T/F), 0.82 (J/P), 0.86 (A/T), and test-retest of 0.83, 0.74, 0.80, 0.79, 0.78 (about 10,000 respondents for alphas; about 2,900 retested after 5-7 months). It reports only discriminant validity, with the largest inter-scale correlation 0.37; no criterion validity is reported. These are self-reported by the test's own publisher. One independent CFA (Makwana and Dave, 1,067 Indian management students) is mentioned in a search summary only. [Weak: publisher-only] (Sources 3, 4, 21)
- **Online community typology** (cognitive-function tests, Ni/Se "stacking" quizzes, forum typing): no psychometric sources found; the function stack is used as an interpretive scheme. Truity is a commercial vendor that describes the stack as contested. [Untested] (Source 9)
- **Test-retest of the official MBTI** (relevant to any function derivation, since the letters feed it): per Wikipedia's summary, 39-76% of people got a different type on retest after about five weeks (Pittenger 1993; others); about 83% of dichotomy categories stayed the same within nine months and about 50% kept the same overall type (Harvey 1996). Scores are centrally peaked rather than bimodal. [Moderate: replicated across studies in the secondary summary, but I did not open the primary papers] (Source 10)

## What proponents argue (the strongest honest version)

- The four scales are reliable, correlate with Big Five factors, and are useful as descriptive self-report categories; the publisher stresses it is not a predictive tool for hiring (Sources 1, 10).
- Type dynamics, on this view, is a clinical and developmental framework for reflection, not a statistical claim; the Foundation presents it as a model for understanding stress and growth (Source 8). Supporters (Nardi, Rowan) answered Reynierse in the type community's own journals (Source 13).
- Direct function instruments such as the SL-TDI show high internal consistency (alpha 0.94 total in one Korean sample) and recovered the intended four dimensions with a bipolar-compatible structure (Source 2).
- Neuroimaging work is exploratory but suggests function-specific patterns that questionnaires could be tested against (Source 17). This is the weakest strand of the evidence.
- Step II is claimed to reduce the one-size-fits-all criticism by adding five facets per scale with a clear four-factor structure (Source 5, publisher-affiliated).

## Open questions a new study could answer

1. In a pre-registered sample with the same respondents completing an official MBTI and an SL-TDI, do the SL-TDI function scores rank in the order the stack predicts (dominant > auxiliary > tertiary > inferior) for more than chance-expected share of participants, after the Reynierse additive-preference model has been fitted as the null?
2. Does a model that predicts outcomes from the four letters as continuous additive scores outperform a model that adds stack-order terms (e.g., Te-dominant vs Ni-dominant) by a pre-specified margin in cross-validated R-squared on a behavioral criterion?
3. Do Nardi-style EEG patterns identified in one lab sample classify function preference above chance in an independent, pre-registered sample of at least 200 participants collected by a different lab, blind to type?
4. Does the SL-TDI S/N dimension (weakest in Lee and Kim: MDS r = 0.192) replicate its weak recovery in other languages and non-convenience samples?
5. Do the 20 Step II facets fit a four-factor second-order structure in an independent, non-publisher sample, with fit indices reported (CFI, RMSEA)?
6. Do 16Personalities' Assertive-Turbulent scores correlate with NEO Neuroticism at a level above the 0.37 maximum inter-scale correlation the company reports, supporting the stated Big-Five derivation?
7. Do blinded socionics experts and MBTI practitioners type the same participants to agree above the roughly 30% overlap claimed in unattributed sources (Source 19)?
